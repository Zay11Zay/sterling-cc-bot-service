# Pitch script — warm contact (electrician)

## The ask (30 seconds)

"Hey [name] — random idea, but I've been building small automation tools
and wanted to try one out on a real business before I start charging
anyone. Quick question: when you're up on a ladder or under a panel and
miss a call, what happens to that lead?"

*(Let him answer — he'll almost always say "they probably just call
someone else" or "I try to call back when I can.")*

"Right — so I built a thing that fixes exactly that: if a call to your
business goes unanswered for 20 seconds, the caller instantly gets a text
saying you're on a job and will call back shortly, so they don't hang up
and Google the next guy. You also get a text with their number so you
know to call back. I'll set the whole thing up for free, you don't pay
for anything, and I just want your honest read on whether it's actually
useful after a couple weeks."

## If he asks "what's the catch"

"No catch — I'm testing whether this is something small businesses would
actually pay for before I try to sell it to strangers. You're my test
case. Worst case it doesn't do anything for you and we turn it back off."

## What you need from him

- His cell number (already have it)
- OK to put a new phone number on his Google Business Profile / website
  as the number customers call (his cell stays private, unaffected)
- Business name as he wants it to appear in the text

## Demo, once it's built

Call the new number in front of him, don't let him answer it, and let the
text land on your phone while he watches. This is the entire sales
pitch — it doesn't need more than that.

## After 2-3 weeks, if he likes it

"If this actually saved you a job or two, I can build out a few more of
these — review requests after a job, reminding people about upcoming
appointments, following up on quotes you sent that went quiet. Want me to
put together a package and a real price for that?"

Don't quote a number yet — that conversation is easier once he's felt the
value, and gives you a real case study ("saved a $400 job in week one")
to use with the next 10 electricians you approach cold.
